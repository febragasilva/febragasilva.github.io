I am currently a third-year undergraduate student at [Stony Brook University]([https://www.stonybrook.edu/]), after completing two years of study at [Anhui University]([https://www.ahu.edu.cn/]). My primary research interest is offline reinforcement learning. As I am still at an early phase of my research journey, I am building a foundation by reproducing basic reinforcement learning algorithms and reading relevant textbooks and research papers. On my GitHub profile, I share study notes and implementations of several foundational algorithms.(This website is still under development.) 

Research Motivation
======
To be honest, I was initially interested in large language models (LLMs). They have developed rapidly and demonstrated remarkable potential.  When I was a teenager, I often encountered difficult questions or became curious about phenomena that I could not explain. Because I was somewhat shy about asking teachers questions, I usually tried to find answers on my own. LLM-powered chatbots appeared to be an ideal tool for this purpose because they could provide immediate explanations and help me explore unfamiliar topics.

Over time, however, I began to distinguish their limitations. LLMs primarily generate responses based on existing data. Although they can combine existing knowledge in useful ways, using an LLM to create something does not necessarily mean that the user understands its underlying mechanism. Their fluent responses may also give people a false sense of confidence, particularly when the models perform well on everyday tasks or introductory questions.

Nevertheless, I believe that LLMs can help reduce educational inequality. I grew up in Huangshan, a city with relatively limited economic and educational resources. People there work extremely hard while often receiving fewer opportunities, and I deeply admire their diligence and optimism toward life. For motivated students who lack access to strong educational support, AI can serve as a patient and accessible tutor.

My interest later shifted toward reinforcement learning. What attracts me to RL is its ability to learn sequential decision-making strategies through interaction and feedback. RL has produced notable results in games, robotics, healthcare, industrial control, and even the training and alignment of LLMs. 

I first encountered reinforcement learning through a video on Bilibili. In the video, a developer used RL to accomplish a task that had previously seemed out of reach. Watching the agent learn through repeated trial and error and eventually succeed was fascinating, and it left a strong impression on me.

Later, I watched videos of RL being applied to robotics. Although many challenges remain and widespread real-world deployment is still a distant goal, RL has the potential to complement or outperform traditional control methods in certain complex tasks. More recently, I have seen developers train RL agents to play online games and livestream the agents’ behavior. Watching these agents learn, fail, and gradually improve is both technically fascinating and genuinely entertaining.

I am particularly interested in offline reinforcement learning because it addresses situations in which real-world experimentation is expensive, risky, or impractical. Instead of repeatedly collecting new samples, an offline RL agent must learn from previously collected experience. Such settings are common in industrial production, healthcare, and other real-world domains where data collection can be difficult or costly. Therefore, I believe that offline RL addresses an important problem with substantial practical value.
